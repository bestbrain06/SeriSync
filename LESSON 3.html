Population Growth

Category: Applied Mathematics  •  Level: Intermediate  •  Duration: ≈ 25 min

Lesson Overview

Population growth is frequently modelled by geometric sequences or exponential functions. When a population increases by a fixed percentage each year, the successive sizes form a geometric sequence. These models help demographers, city planners and environmental scientists forecast future needs for housing, food, schools and infrastructure.

Learning Objectives

  • Recognise geometric growth in population data.
  • Calculate population size after a given number of time periods.
  • Determine the annual growth rate from successive population figures.
  • Discuss the limitations of simple geometric models in real-world situations.

Mathematical Examples

Example 1 – Town Population Growth

A town currently has 12 000 residents and grows by 3% each year.
What will the population be after 8 years?

This is a geometric sequence with:
  first term  P0 = 12 000
  common ratio  r = 1.03

General term:
Pn = 12 000 × (1.03)n
Population after 8 years:
P8 = 12 000 × (1.03)8 ≈ 12 000 × 1.2668 = 15 201
Example 2 – Finding the Growth Rate

A city's population rose from 8 500 to 9 265 in one year.
What is the annual growth rate?

Growth factor = new population⁄old population
Growth factor = 9265⁄8500 = 1.09
Annual growth rate = 9%
Example 3 – Predicting When a Target is Reached

A city has 250 000 people and grows at 2.5% per year.
In which year will the population first exceed 350 000?

We solve:
250 000 × (1.025)n > 350 000
(1.025)n > 350000⁄250000 = 1.4
Taking logarithms (or testing values):
  n = 14 → (1.025)14 ≈ 1.413 > 1.4
The population first exceeds 350 000 during the 14th year.

Practice Activity

Your Turn

1. A village has 4 800 inhabitants and grows at 2% per year.
  • Write the general term for the population after n years.
  • Estimate the population after 10 years.

2. A town's population increased from 15 200 to 16 112 in one year.
  • Calculate the annual growth rate as a percentage.

3. A city's current population is 180 000 and it grows at 3.2% per year.
  • In approximately which year will the population first exceed 250 000?
  • Discuss one limitation of using a constant growth rate model for long-term predictions.
