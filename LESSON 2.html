Saving and Investment Plans

Category: Financial Mathematics  •  Level: Intermediate  •  Duration: ≈ 30 min

Lesson Overview

Compound interest and regular savings plans are perfect applications of geometric sequences and series. When money grows at a fixed percentage rate, each new amount is obtained by multiplying the previous amount by a constant factor. Understanding these models helps you plan for the future, compare investment options and make informed financial decisions.

Learning Objectives

  • Model compound interest using geometric sequences.
  • Calculate the future value of a single investment and of regular deposits (annuities).
  • Compare different savings and investment strategies.
  • Solve real-life problems involving interest rates and time periods.

Mathematical Examples

Example 1 – Compound Interest (Single Deposit)

You invest $5 000 in an account that pays 6% interest per year, compounded annually.
How much will the investment be worth after 5 years?

This is a geometric sequence with:
  first term  P = 5000
  common ratio  r = 1.06

Future value formula:
A = P(1 + r)n
A = 5000 × (1.06)5 ≈ 5000 × 1.3382 = $6 691.13
Example 2 – Regular Monthly Savings (Annuity)

You deposit $200 at the end of every month into an account that earns 0.5% interest per month.
How much will you have after 2 years (24 deposits)?

This is the sum of a geometric series:
Sn = R × (1 + r)n − 1⁄r
where R = 200, r = 0.005, n = 24.

S24 = 200 × (1.005)24 − 1⁄0.005 ≈ 200 × 25.478 = $5 095.60
Example 3 – Finding the Interest Earned

In Example 2 you deposited a total of 24 × $200 = $4 800.
The final amount was approximately $5 095.60.

Interest earned:
Interest = Final Amount − Total Deposits = 5095.60 − 4800 = $295.60

Practice Activity

Your Turn

1. You invest $8 000 at 5% per year compounded annually.
  • How much will it be worth after 6 years?
  • How much interest will you have earned?

2. You save $150 at the end of every month in an account that pays 0.4% interest per month.
  • How much will you have after 3 years (36 deposits)?
  • How much of that amount is interest?

3. Compare two plans:
  • Plan A: $5 000 invested once at 6% p.a. for 5 years.
  • Plan B: $100 deposited every month at 0.5% per month for 5 years.
Which plan gives the larger final amount? Show your calculations.
