Introduction to Real Life Applications of Sequence and Series

Category: Sequences & Series  •  Level: Intermediate  •  Duration: ≈ 25 min

Lesson Overview

Sequences and series are mathematical patterns that appear everywhere in daily life. An arithmetic sequence increases or decreases by a constant difference, while a geometric sequence multiplies by a constant ratio. Understanding these patterns helps us model theatre seating, bouncing balls, savings plans, population growth and many other real-world situations.

Learning Objectives

  • Define arithmetic and geometric sequences and series.
  • Identify real-life situations that follow sequence patterns.
  • Apply the general term and sum formulas to practical problems.
  • Connect classroom concepts to finance, nature and design.

Mathematical Examples

Example 1 – Theatre Seating (Arithmetic Sequence)

A theatre has 20 seats in the first row. Each following row has 4 more seats than the previous one.

This forms an arithmetic sequence with:
  first term  a = 20
  common difference  d = 4

General term:
an = 20 + (n − 1) × 4
Number of seats in the 10th row:
a10 = 20 + (10 − 1) × 4 = 20 + 36 = 56 seats
Example 2 – Bouncing Ball (Geometric Sequence)

A ball is dropped from a height of 16 m. Each bounce reaches ¾ of the previous height.

Heights form a geometric sequence: 16, 12, 9, 6.75, …
  first term  a = 16
  common ratio  r = ¾

General term:
an = 16 × (¾)n−1
Height after the 4th bounce:
a5 = 16 × (¾)4 = 16 × 81⁄256 = 5.0625 m
Example 3 – Sum of an Arithmetic Series

Find the total number of seats in the first 10 rows of the theatre.

Sum of the first n terms of an arithmetic series:
Sn = n⁄2 [2a + (n − 1)d]
or
Sn = n⁄2 (a + l)
where l is the last term.

Using the first formula:
S10 = 10⁄2 [2 × 20 + (10 − 1) × 4] = 5 [40 + 36] = 5 × 76 = 380 seats

Practice Activity

Your Turn

1. A stadium has 15 seats in the first row and each next row has 3 more seats.
  • Write the general term an.
  • How many seats are in the 12th row?
  • Find the total number of seats in the first 12 rows.

2. A rubber ball is dropped from 10 m and rebounds to ⅔ of its previous height each time.
  • Write the first four heights as a geometric sequence.
  • Find the height after the 5th bounce using the general term.

3. List two real-life situations that can be modelled by an arithmetic sequence and two that can be modelled by a geometric sequence. Explain your choices briefly.
